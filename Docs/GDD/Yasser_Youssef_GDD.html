1. Storyline Summary

Wulang: Elements of the Past is a first-person action-adventure about a woman who is told who she was, and has to decide whether to believe it.

The Vale was kept by four elemental guardians, Earth, Frost, Flame and the Storm, who asked nothing for what they gave: harvests, long winters, warm hearths and rain. Then someone began to put a price on them. Book-bearers carried sealed tomes into their glades, ledgers that wrote down what each guardian was worth, and one by one the guardians soured into monsters. The Storm alone escaped. Fleeing across the sky, it hid inside the only person out on the hill road above Lowmere: a baker's granddaughter walking home in the rain with bread under her coat.

She wakes at an abandoned armory with lightning in her hands and no memory. A kindly old armorer named Abe tells her she is Wulang, that he raised her, and that the elements are proud: they cannot be stolen or bought, only given. If she puts the sick guardians to rest, their gifts will pass to her. Abe makes terrible tea, sells her potions, knows every guardian's weakness, and is always waiting at the next camp before she gets there.

Each element Wulang claims returns a memory, and every memory puts Abe at its centre: Abe teaching her to hear the soil, Abe pulling her from a frozen lake, Abe making her swear that what is given freely cannot be taken, and that one day she will give the elements to the one who raised her. The memories are warm and convincing, but each has something wrong in it. Young Wulang says the soil is telling her the soup is boiling over, and Abe corrects her. The ice water is warm. She hums a song Abe has never heard. Off the main path, notes, plaques and ledgers fill in what Abe leaves out: a patron who paid in old silver for every tome and never touched one, a children's rhyme about a Tallyman who wears a friend's face, and a memorial in Lowmere to a baker's granddaughter who went out for bread and never came home.

When all four gifts burn in her hands, a rift opens on a sanctum in the sky, and Abe is there ahead of her, as always. He makes his offer: give him the elements, freely, and he will give her back a past worth having, a great name and a life that mattered. Every memory so far was a down payment. If she kneels, she receives exactly that: a perfect past that is not hers, and the Vale's rain stops falling for free. If she refuses, Abe stops pretending. The disguise burns away and Ganfaul, the Tallyman, takes up the stolen elements one at a time; only answering each one with its weakness brings him down.

Beating him releases Wulang's real memory, and it is small: a wet evening, her grandmother's rain song, bread under her coat. No destiny and no debt. The tone moves from warm and curious, to uneasy, to defiant. The core conflict is between a past that is sold to you and a life you own: Wulang wins not by becoming a legend but by refusing to be priced, and by giving the gifts back to the land that gave them.

(Summary: 560 words.)

2. Characters

Each profile follows the lecture's character background paper and story-driven character design: role, values, personality and behaviour, characterization and status attributes, growth, and audio design (vocabulary, speed and tone). Portraits are rendered in-engine from the game's own models and appear in the dialogue box.


Wulang
protagonist

Background: a baker's granddaughter from the hill village of Lowmere; her real life is bread at dawn, rain walks and her Nan's song. The fleeing Storm struck her on the hill road and took her memory. Role: the player character and the one person the elements accept. Motivation: to find out who she was, and then to decide who she is. Personality: dry, wary humour ("And you're sending me, because...?"), polite, quick to notice small wrong details. In every memory she says yes to Abe; her growth (character dimension) is learning to say no. Status attributes: health, potions, Wubucks, the elements she carries (the element ring), her journal. Audio: short plain sentences, understatement, never speeches. Relation to the player: in first person, player and Wulang start with the same blank; her journal fills as the player explores, so her doubts become the player's.


Abe
mentor, merchant

Background: claims to be the armorer who raised her; is Ganfaul in disguise, a false mentor in the vein of the disguised god in God of War Ragnarök. Role: the Hero's Journey mentor and helper: advice on each guardian's weakness, potions for Wubucks, safe camps where enemies give up the chase. Motivation: says he wants the guardians at rest; needs the elements, which can only be given. Personality and behaviour: warm, gruff, funny, sits by the fire gesturing as he talks, haggles ("Nothing's free, spark. Not even me."), never touches a tome, and is always at the next camp first with dry boots. Audio: unhurried and folksy; calls her "spark"; talks in prices, debts and down payments, which sounds like a merchant's habit until the Sanctum. Relation to the player: the tracker's first objective points at him; the player's trust in him is the material the climax turns over.


Ganfaul
antagonist

Background: "the Tallyman" of a children's rhyme, who weighs the rain and counts the sand. He sent the book-bearers whose ledgers soured the guardians, but cannot take what can only be given. Role: the final boss and the voice behind the offer; his horned shadow falls from Abe's body at the last camp. Motivation: to own what was never for sale. Personality: patient, transactional, contemptuous of free gifts. Mechanic as character: he fights with every stolen element in turn, and only the current element's weakness hurts him. Last words: "Freely... given. I never could afford that."

The Guardians (supporting characters and bosses): the Earth Guardian (Blossom Glade), the Ice Guardian (Frozen Amphitheater) and the Fire Guardian (the caldera) are corrupted, not evil. Each speaks once when the fight begins and once dying, and the dying words are requests that become clues: "give it back when you are done", "ask who paid for the books", "when they offer you a past, ask what it costs". Nan never appears on screen: she lives in the rain-song melody of the score, the shrine plaque and a child's line carved in the ice.

4. Level Descriptions

Each chapter is a streamed Unity scene loaded over the persistent world, so the next level is always visible on the horizon and is entered through a story-gated transition rather than a menu.

4.1 Chapter I, The Spark: the Forest (tutorial)

Narrative role: the hook. The intro establishes the premise, Abe establishes the rules ("they only go where they're given"), and the first guardian and first memory plant the complication. Environmental storytelling: the Earth guardian's rampage (broken weapon racks, an overturned wagon, barrels) is strewn beside the trail and leads the player to the Blossom Glade without a marker; a crown of runestones states the old law, "let no one hand hold them all", which Abe's mission quietly breaks; an abandoned book-bearer's camp with scattered tomes; a shrine plaque for the baker's granddaughter who never came home. A warm colour grade, birdsong and the forest theme make it feel safe. Mechanics that reinforce the story: Wulang starts with only the Storm's gift, and the Earth guardian is weak to Lightning; claiming Earth wakes the ice statue, whose freeze reveal turns the lake into the road to Chapter II.

4.2 Chapter II, What the Cold Keeps: the Frozen Reach

Narrative role: the hold begins. Abe is already at the frozen bank with dry boots; the second memory adds a debt ("one day I'll ask you for something"). Environmental storytelling: travellers frozen solid, all facing away from the Amphitheater, as if caught running; a frozen caravan whose manifest was "paid in full, in old silver"; ice-carved verse, "the cold keeps what the warm forget", with a child's line about Nan beneath it; a desaturated blue grade, howling wind and the absence of birds. Mechanic: the Ice guardian shrugs off single elements, so Wulang combines two gifts (Earth then Lightning, the Storm Quake) to stagger it: carrying more than one gift is what makes her valuable to Abe.

4.3 Chapter III, The Price of Fire: the Burning Peak

Narrative role: rising dread. Abe promises to "tell you everything you were" after the last guardian; Wulang asks whether she ever said no to him. Environmental storytelling: the burnt watch post (an old man with a kettle walking behind the screaming guardian); the chain forge's order for forty chains "in case of a refusal"; an ember shrine's prayer ("he was kind, and he paid us well"); Abe's horned shadow beside the fire; Ganfaul's toppled idol and chains at the rift. Ember-orange contrast, lava rumble and crackle. Mechanic: heat and lava hazards; the route runs through a lava tube onto a causeway; Ice tames Fire, so the gift taken from the cold is what opens the mountain.

4.4 Chapter IV, The Sanctum

Narrative role: the payoff. A floating ring of stone with four element pillars, Abe standing at its heart. Environmental storytelling: each pillar lights as Ganfaul takes up its element; the violet grade and a detuned version of the rain song make the familiar wrong. Mechanics: the meaningful choice (kneel or refuse); the reveal cutscene; a phase-shifting boss that resists everything except his current weakness, so the player must use every gift they earned; dying twice brings the offer back, so the temptation is a gameplay pressure, not only a line of dialogue.

Dialogue format: ScriptableObjects. Each conversation is one DialogueData asset (lines with speaker, portrait and text; optional choices, each with a flag to set; a flag set when finished). Assets are referenced directly by triggers, NPCs and endings in the Inspector, so there is no string lookup to break, and they version cleanly in Git. Data, logic and display are separate: DialogueData, DialogueManager (queue, typewriter, choices, sets flags) and DialogueUI. The writing itself is kept in one authoring file, StoryScript.cs, whose Apply story script command writes it into the assets and scenes, so the whole script can be read and revised in one place while the game only reads data.

Narrative and supporting scripts (commented): StoryStateManager, DialogueManager, DialogueUI, ObjectiveManager, RegionObjectives, NarrativeTrigger, MemoryEcho, IntroCutscene, EndingController; level transitions: RegionManager, RegionTravel, FlagGate, RegionStart; UI: MainMenu, PauseMenu, InventoryUI, TransitionOverlay; player: FirstPersonController, SpellCaster, PlayerHealth; audio: GameAudio.

7. Art and Aesthetic

Visual style: a stylized, saturated fantasy (Idyllic Fantasy Nature, Stylized Rock Pack, Stylized Fantasy Armory, Cartoon Sky) whose palette is pushed per chapter so colour carries the mood: a warm, golden forest; a cold, desaturated blue reach; an ember-black volcano; a violet sanctum. Each region scene brings its own colour-grade volume, so the world visibly changes as the chapter changes. Memories drain the colour to a blue-grey with a vignette, and ghosts of Wulang and Abe act them out. Characters are Mixamo models with root-motion animation; the portraits are rendered from the same models.

Audio style: music and ambience change with the region, a boss fight, a memory and each ending. A single melody, Nan's rain song, ties the score together: it opens the menu and the intro, plays thin and slightly out of tune under Abe's memories, returns detuned in the Sanctum, and is played whole and warm only in the Truth ending; the Gilded Past ending sets it in a hollow minor key. Ambience beds (birdsong, wind, lava rumble, sanctum air) and effects (casts, impacts, roars, chests, thunder) support the lecture's "satisfaction". The current music and ambience are synthesised placeholders, made by a script so they can be replaced file by file.
