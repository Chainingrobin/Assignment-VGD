High conceptA first-person elemental action-adventure in which an amnesiac woman frees four sick guardians and claims their gifts, while the kindly mentor who "returns" her memories is the one who wants the gifts for himself. Her real past is small, and it is hers.
Genre (hybrid)Action-adventure (Lecture 2: genre = type of challenge): physical coordination and conflict challenges (real-time spell combat) with exploration and conceptual-reasoning challenges (finding and reading clues), held together by an authored story.
Player role and verbsThe player is Wulang, a character with her own voice, not a blank avatar (Lecture 5). Verbs: explore, cast (projectile and area), switch element, combine elements, talk, read, trade, drink potions, choose.

Player types (Lecture 2): Explorers (nine off-path points of interest, journal clues) and Achievers (four guardians, six vitality relics, New Game+). Story Mode serves players who mainly want the story (Lecture 4).

1. Storyline Summary

Wulang: Elements of the Past is a first-person action-adventure about a woman who is told who she was, and has to decide whether to believe it.

The Vale was kept by four elemental guardians, Earth, Frost, Flame and the Storm, who asked nothing for what they gave: harvests, long winters, warm hearths and rain. Then someone began to put a price on them. Book-bearers carried sealed tomes into their glades, ledgers that wrote down what each guardian was worth, and one by one the guardians soured into monsters. The Storm alone escaped. Fleeing across the sky, it hid inside the only person out on the hill road above Lowmere: a baker's granddaughter walking home in the rain with bread under her coat.

She wakes at an abandoned armory with lightning in her hands and no memory. A kindly old armorer named Abe tells her she is Wulang, that he raised her, and that the elements are proud: they cannot be stolen or bought, only given. If she puts the sick guardians to rest, their gifts will pass to her. Abe makes terrible tea, sells her potions, knows every guardian's weakness, and is always waiting at the next camp before she gets there.

Each element Wulang claims returns a memory, and every memory puts Abe at its centre: Abe teaching her to hear the soil, Abe pulling her from a frozen lake, Abe making her swear that what is given freely cannot be taken, and that one day she will give the elements to the one who raised her. The memories are warm and convincing, but each has something wrong in it. Young Wulang says the soil is telling her the soup is boiling over, and Abe corrects her. The ice water is warm. She hums a song Abe has never heard. Off the main path, every chest and note returns a short fragment of her own life as a reward for exploring: her grandmother Nan, who called her "Wu"; the village of Lowmere; a cup of snow left out for the Frost guardian; bread under her coat on market day; a skipping rhyme about a Tallyman; a patron who paid in old silver for every tome and never touched one.

When all four gifts burn in her hands, a rift opens on a sanctum in the sky, and Abe is there ahead of her, as always. He makes his offer: give him the elements, freely, and he will give her back a past worth having, a great name and a life that mattered. Every memory so far was a down payment. If she kneels, she receives exactly that: a perfect past that is not hers, and the Vale's rain stops falling for free. If she refuses, Abe stops pretending. The disguise burns away and Ganfaul, the Tallyman, takes up the stolen elements one at a time; only answering each one with its weakness brings him down.

Beating him releases Wulang's real memory, and it is small: a wet evening, her grandmother's rain song, bread under her coat. No destiny and no debt. The tone moves from warm and curious, to uneasy, to defiant. The core conflict is between a past that is sold to you and a life you own: Wulang wins not by becoming a legend but by refusing to be priced, and by giving the gifts back to the land that gave them.

(Summary: 553 words. Premise: paragraphs 2 and 3. Tone: warm and curious, then uneasy, then defiant. Core conflict: a bought past against an owned life.)

Story spine (Lecture 5): Once, the Vale's guardians gave freely, until someone started counting what they were worth and the Storm hid in a girl on the hill road. Because of that, she woke with no past and an old armorer ready to sell her one, one memory per freed guardian. Until finally, he asked for the gifts in return; she refused to be priced, and her past turned out to be small, and hers.

2. Game World and Setting

The Vale is one 1 km × 1 km terrain (1 unit = 1 m, 600 m of height) whose four regions are the four chapters. The bond between world and story (Lecture 3) is literal: each region is a guardian's domain, and world state is story state (the lake freezes only when the Earth guardian rests). The setting gives premise and geography; the story gives direction and progression (Lecture 5: setting vs. story).

Dimension (Lecture 3)Wulang's world
Physical: spatialContinuous 3D, first-person camera (Cinemachine). Grounded movement (walk 4 m/s, sprint 7 m/s, jump), airborne spells. Space is essential to play: range, cover in arenas, routes and hazards.
Physical: scaleRealistic for people and props (2 m player capsule, life-size armory and camps); exaggerated for the guardians, which tower over Wulang to create awe; the Sanctum is a floating island. One scale indoors and out.
Physical: boundariesMountain ranges enclose the Vale (containment). A locked region is sealed by its element: the seal pushes Wulang back with a short line ("The lake won't hold me. Not yet."), so no invisible-wall text is needed. The unfrozen lake drowns (damage, then back to shore) and lava burns. Boundaries open as the story does (exploration, Lecture 3).
TemporalA full day and night takes 8 real minutes (sun, moon, stars, ambient light). Time is atmosphere, not pressure: no time limits, hunger or seasons, and enemies and shops behave the same at night. Time is skipped during chapter flights. Anomalous time: the memory echoes are flashbacks played in place (colour drains, ghosts act the scene). The player cannot adjust time; the pause menu stops it.
Environmental and culturalAn invented, pre-industrial valley. People live in hill villages such as Lowmere (bakers, millers); book-bearers carried tomes for a patron who "paid in old silver". Values: what is given freely (rain, harvest, warmth) against what is priced (ledgers, debts, down payments). Surroundings: a green forest and armory compound, a glacier reach with a frozen caravan, a burning peak with a chain forge, a sanctum in the sky. Detail: dense around landmarks, quiet along trails (areas of rest vs. detail). Inspiration: Breath of the Wild (regions and landmarks), God of War Ragnarök (a god disguised as a mentor).
EmotionalWonder and warmth (Forest), unease and loneliness (Frozen Reach), suspicion and dread (Burning Peak), betrayal, defiance and quiet relief (Sanctum). Relayed by colour grades, lighting, music (the rain song) and Abe's warmth. At stake: Wulang's identity, and whether the Vale's rain stays free.
EthicalRight is giving freely; wrong is pricing what was given. Conflict is stylised elemental magic against corrupted creatures; the guardians are put to rest, and their last words are warnings, not curses. The player never harms a person. Kneeling is never a game over, only a hollow ending; the "good" outcome requires refusing, and Ganfaul can only be beaten by using each weakness in turn (not every means wins).

7. Storyline Implementation Plan

Story state lives in StoryStateManager: a DontDestroyOnLoad set of string flags with an OnFlagSet event, checkpointed on every region arrival for Continue. Triggers set flags; objectives, seals, exits, dialogue and endings read them. Every trigger fires once (its own flag) and only when its required flag is set.

7.4 Dialogue format: ScriptableObjects (data, not code)

All story text is data that is edited in the Unity Inspector; no line of dialogue is written in a C# script. Conversations: one DialogueData ScriptableObject per conversation (11 in Assets/Story/Dialogue): lines with speaker, portrait and text, optional choices that each set a flag and can link to another conversation, and a flag set when finished. In the lecture's terms this is a dialogue tree: lines are nodes, choices are links, and preconditions (a trigger's required flag) and actions (set a flag, give a journal page, open the next conversation) are story flags. Journal pages, memories and item lore: one ItemData asset each (21). Everything else (guardian lines, chapter cards and recaps, objectives, barks, ending cards): one StoryText asset, Assets/Story/Story Text.asset, copied onto the scenes by Wulang > Story > Apply story text. Why: ScriptableObjects are the format the assignment guide recommends; they are edited in the Inspector without touching code (conversation and journal edits apply immediately, even in Play mode), referenced directly by triggers so no string lookup can break, and they diff cleanly in Git. Data (DialogueData), logic (DialogueManager: queue, typewriter, choices, flags) and display (DialogueUI) are separate.

7.5 Scripts, level transitions and testing

Documented scripts (class and member documentation comments): narrative: StoryStateManager, DialogueData, DialogueManager, ObjectiveManager, RegionObjectives, NarrativeTrigger, StoryText; level transitions: RegionManager, RegionTravel, FlagGate; UI interaction: DialogueUI, InventoryUI; player controls: PlayerInteract. Also MemoryEcho, IntroCutscene, EndingController, MainMenu, PauseMenu, TransitionOverlay, SpellCaster, PlayerHealth, GameAudio. Transitions are conditioned on story state, not position: walking into a region works only when its flag is set (otherwise its seal pushes back); the waystone and rift are switched on by a FlagGate; each loads with SceneManager.LoadSceneAsync under a chapter card with a recap, and the state carries over because the story manager is never unloaded. Cutscenes (intro, flights, freeze reveal, memories, Ganfaul's reveal) use Cinemachine cameras and Timeline, can all be skipped with Space, and set a flag when they finish. Testing the full path (guide Step 11): Wulang > Checks > Full story path plays from the menu through every beat to both endings, checking that each beat fires once and in order and that the objective matches the story state at every step.

8. Art and Aesthetic

Visual style: a stylised, saturated fantasy (Idyllic Fantasy Nature, Stylized Rock Pack, Stylized Fantasy Armory, Cartoon Sky) with one cohesive look whose palette shifts per chapter so colour carries the tone (Lecture 5, style): a golden forest, a cold blue reach, an ember-black volcano, a violet sanctum. Audio style: region music and ambience that change for fights, memories and endings, tied together by one melody, Nan's rain song: thin and out of tune under Abe's memories, detuned in the Sanctum, whole and warm only in the Truth ending.

Contrast (value, saturation)Glowing lava and lit chests against dark rock; light beams over every reward; gold UI on dark backings.LightingDay and night, spell lights, the blue freeze front, the Sanctum's pillar lights; memories drain to blue-grey with a vignette.
Shape and sizeThe guardians' exaggerated silhouettes read as danger at a glance.Level of detailConcentrated at landmarks; trails are areas of rest.
VFX (particle systems)Each element has its own projectile, area spell and impact.Sound and satisfactionAmbience beds (birdsong, wind, lava rumble, sanctum air); effects (casts, impacts, roars, chests, thunder); the Gilded Past ending sets the rain song in a hollow minor key. Music and ambience are synthesised placeholders, replaceable file by file.
